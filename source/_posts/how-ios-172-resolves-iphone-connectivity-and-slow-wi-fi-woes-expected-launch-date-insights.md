## How iOS 17.2 Resolves iPhone Connectivity and Slow Wi-Fi Woes – Expected Launch Date Insights

![iPhone 15 Pro photo taken with iPhone 15 Pro Max in portrait mode](https://www.zdnet.com/a/img/resize/cbb6e06548207ca48ddc17f21c5a95be254f99ba/2023/10/05/c175a0e5-2ef1-400f-b90b-8dd3cf353de4/img-1927.jpg?auto=webp&width=1280)

Jason Hiner/ZDNET

iOS 17.1 did a good job of destroying a lot of the bugs that have been plaguing iPhone users since the release of the new version in September. The [overheating problems](https://www.zdnet.com/article/iphone-15-overheating-heres-what-you-should-do/) that made iPhones [almost too hot to touch](https://www.zdnet.com/article/new-iphone-15-pro-overheating-reports-still-too-hot-after-ios-17-0-3-and-fresh-issues-arise-after-the-update/) seem to be fixed, as is that [odd screen burn-in problem that wasn't burn-in](https://www.zdnet.com/article/your-new-iphone-15-suffering-from-screen-burn-in-apple-has-some-good-news-for-you/). As for that [obscure nighttime rebooting bug](https://www.zdnet.com/article/mystery-bug-causes-iphones-to-turn-off-at-night/), I'm not sure about that one.

Oh, and you can [still use a Flipper Zero to crash an iPhone](https://www.zdnet.com/article/ios-17-1-update-still-no-defense-against-flipper-zero-iphone-crashes/).

**Also: [Everything Apple announced at its October 'Scary Fast' event (and what wasn't unveiled)](https://www.zdnet.com/article/everything-apple-announced-at-its-october-scary-fast-event-m3-macbook-pro-24-inch-imac-more/)**

But there's another bug that has been causing users headaches, and that's related to poor Wi-Fi performance.

This bug doesn't seem to be widespread, but those affected by it are suffering from slow Wi-Fi speeds and dropped connections. The "fix" was to turn Wi-Fi off and back on again, and let the connection reestablish, although this was only a temporary fix.

Well, according to [_iClarified_](https://www.iclarified.com/91867/apple-says-ios-172-beta-fixes-wifi-issues), there's good news and bad news.

The good news is that a fix is coming in the form of iOS 17.2.

**Also: [iOS 17.1 update still no defense against Flipper Zero iPhone crashes](https://www.zdnet.com/article/ios-17-1-update-still-no-defense-against-flipper-zero-iphone-crashes/)**

The bad news is that [we've just had iOS 17.1](https://www.zdnet.com/article/iphone-users-who-dont-want-to-be-tracked-need-apples-ios-17-1-privacy-patch/), and iOS 17.2 is currently in beta. It's going to be December before we see this land of iPhones.

That's possibly a few more weeks of Wi-Fi headaches for those affected.

That said, I have seen bug fixes slated for .x releases rolled into smaller updates in the past, so if Apple needs to push out updates to iOS 17.1 before rolling out iOS 17.2, this Wi-Fi fix might see the light of day sooner.

**Also: [iPhone users who don't want to be tracked need Apple's iOS 17.1 privacy patch](https://www.zdnet.com/article/iphone-users-who-dont-want-to-be-tracked-need-apples-ios-17-1-privacy-patch/)**

Oh, and it's also unclear at this point if this is a complete fix -- we'll just have to wait and see.

Along with the usual slew of bug fixes and security patches, iOS 17.2 will bring with it a bunch of new features that have been promised, such as a [Journal app](https://www.zdnet.com/article/apples-new-journal-app-is-coming-to-iphone-everything-you-need-to-know/), a Translate feature for the iPhone 15 Pro's [Action button](https://www.zdnet.com/article/how-to-use-the-iphone-15-pros-new-action-button-to-do-just-about-anything/), and the iMessage Contact Key Verification security option to strengthen messaging privacy, amongst other things.
